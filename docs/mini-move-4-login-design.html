MINI MOVE 4 · UX DESIGN

Sign in with Google

The new front door to balladharma — one calm button, every state accounted for. Screen and interaction design only; no code.

Builds on: the approved Requirements step (docs/mini-move-4-auth-requirements.md). This design realises Requirements §4 Story 3 (refusal), Story 4 (route protection), Story 6 (old journeys gone) and §8 (login reworked to a single Google button; signup / forgot-password / reset-password retired). No real user emails appear anywhere — placeholders only, per §6.

DESIGN INTENT

Serene, not corporate

The login screen keeps the app's contemplative character — the gold enō brush mark on deep slate, generous stillness, one clear action. The only new element is Google's own button, which by Google's brand rules must stay recognisable (four-colour "G", the exact words "Sign in with Google"). We honour that convention (Jakob's Law) while letting everything around it breathe.

EVERY STATE

The five states of the login screen

Each panel is the same screen at phone width (~375px). A person should always know what just happened, what to do next, and how to get back on track. Nothing relies on colour alone — every refusal and error pairs an icon with a heading and plain words.

1

Default — resting

Welcome back

Continue your practice

Sign in with Google

balladharma is invite-only while we’re in early access. Ask Nick if you’d like to join.

One action, nothing else. The old email, password, "Forgot password?" and "Create an account" have all gone. A quiet invite line replaces them (see “Removed links” below).

2

Signing in — redirecting

Welcome back

Continue your practice

Connecting to Google…

Taking you to Google to confirm it’s you. This only takes a moment.

The button becomes its own progress indicator and is no longer clickable — no separate disabled-looking control with no explanation. Announced to screen readers via aria-live.

3

Refused — not on the list

You don’t have access yet

balladharma is open to a small group of invited practitioners right now, and you@example.com isn’t on the list. If you think it should be, ask Nick to add you.

Plain English, no jargon, no blame. Icon + heading + text carry the meaning together — not colour. Shows the email that was tried so the person knows which account was refused. Clear recovery: sign in with a different account.

4

Something went wrong

We couldn’t finish signing you in

The connection to Google was interrupted. This is usually temporary. Check you’re online and try again.

Covers Google auth failing, a network drop, or a timeout. It names what happened and what to do — and the primary way forward is simply to try the same button again.

5

Signed in — arriving

Settling in…

A brief, calm hand-off as the dashboard loads behind a soft veil, then the person lands on /dashboard. Signed-in visitors who return to the login page or landing page are sent straight here — they never see the sign-in screen again while signed in.

TRIGGER → COMPLETION

The interaction flow, including recovery

One happy path, two ways to fall off it, and a clear way back from each. This mirrors the mechanism in Requirements §5.

  1. Person opens the login screen (directly, or bounced here from a protected page while signed out).
    Sees the enō mark, “Welcome back”, and a single Google button.
  2. Taps “Sign in with Google” — the button turns into a “Connecting to Google…” progress state and they’re handed to Google’s own consent screen.
  3. Returns from Google. The app quietly checks: is this email on the invite list and a known account?
    If not → State 3. No session is created. Plain-English “you don’t have access yet”, with the option to try another account. (Matches Requirements §6: an unknown or un-invited email is cleanly refused, never left half-signed-in.)
    If something breaks along the way (Google error, dropped connection) → State 4, with a “Try again” button.
  4. Recognised. The person is signed in and gently handed to their dashboard (State 5), where all their past sessions, journal entries and settings are exactly as they left them.
  5. Later, they sign out → returned to the public landing page, session cleared. Visiting login or landing again while signed in sends them straight back to the dashboard.

WHAT REPLACES THE OLD LINKS

Removed journeys, handled gracefully

Requirements §8 retires signup, forgot-password and reset-password. A dead link is worse than no link, so each is either replaced by a plain-English line or simply removed because Google now owns that job.

“Create an account”

New to balladharma? Create an account

Replaced by a quiet invite note: “balladharma is invite-only while we’re in early access. Ask Nick if you’d like to join.” No public signup exists, so we set the expectation honestly instead of offering a button that goes nowhere.

“Forgot password?”

Forgot password? → reset flow

Removed entirely. There is no balladharma password any more — Google handles identity, and password recovery lives with Google. Nothing on our screen needs to mention it.

Signup / reset-password pages

/signup   /forgot-password   /reset-password

Gone. Anyone reaching those old addresses is redirected to the login screen, so an old bookmark or emailed link still lands somewhere sensible rather than on a “page not found”.

Note for the landing page: its two “Start Your Practice” / “Create Free Account” buttons currently point at signup. During invite-only they should point at the login screen (Sign in with Google) or be softened to an invite message — flagged for the build step, as the landing copy sits just outside this screen’s scope.

QUALITY FLOOR

Accessibility & responsiveness

  • Touch targets ≥ 48px. The Google button and every recovery control clear Fitts’s Law comfortably on a phone.
  • Never colour alone. Refusal and error each pair an icon and a bold heading with the text, so the meaning survives for colour-blind and low-vision users.
  • Contrast passes AA. Foreground #e0e4eb on #1a1f2e is ~12:1; the white Google button with #1f1f1f text is ~16:1; gold #c9a84c on slate clears 3:1 for large text and UI edges.
  • Keyboard & screen reader. Visible gold focus ring (the app’s existing :focus-visible style), status messages use aria-live / role="alert", and the busy button announces progress rather than silently disabling.
  • Works at 375px and in both themes. The screen is a single centred column that reflows cleanly; all tokens come from the app’s light/dark CSS variables, so light mode follows automatically.
  • Reduced motion respected. The enō draw and spinners only animate when the viewer hasn’t asked for reduced motion.
